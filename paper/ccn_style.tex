\section{Abstract}
{
\bf
\looseness=-1
We ask how model collapse affects in-context learning (ICL) and the induction circuit that implements it. We train two-layer transformers (103k parameters) on an adaptation of Singh et al.'s symbol--label task and retrain them for 6--8 generations on their predecessor's outputs. When the model predicts only the query label, query accuracy stays at 1.000 in every generation. When it also predicts a next symbol, the next-symbol distribution degrades (KL on real data 0.09$\to$0.52 nats, median of five seeds) while query accuracy stays $\geq$0.999. This prediction does not use the induction pathway: the path into layer-1 keys that carries 99\% of the previous-token head's effect on the query label leaves it unchanged, and the induction head matters for it no more than the other layer-1 heads. We split the KL exactly into \emph{leakage}, probability on symbols outside the context, which filtering and longer training reduce, and \emph{misallocation} among symbols in the context, which neither reduces. At temperature 0.7, sampling accounts for most of the narrowing up to generation 4, after which the models narrow less than sampling predicts. Models that write their own contexts still copy labels correctly. In this setting, collapse degrades a prediction the induction pathway does not make and leaves the pathway intact.
}

\section{Introduction} \label{Intro}
\looseness=-1
Much of ICL is carried by \emph{induction heads}: at a repeated token $A$, a layer-1 head attends to whatever followed the earlier $A$ and copies it, finding that position through a key written by a layer-0 \emph{previous-token head} \citep{elhage2021mathematical,olsson2022context}. \citet{singh2024needs} dissected how this circuit forms on arbitrary but consistent symbol--label pairs. \emph{Model collapse} is the degradation of models trained recursively on their predecessors' outputs: errors compound and the tails of the distribution vanish \citep{shumailov2023curse,shumailov2024collapse}, unless real data is kept \citep{gerstgrasser2024inevitable}. Its effect on the \emph{mechanisms} inside a model is unknown. We track both the circuit and the collapsing behaviour with causal interventions \citep{conmy2023automated} and data deletions.

\section{Methods}
\looseness=-1
\textbf{Task and model.} Singh et al. used Omniglot exemplars, two pairs and attention-only models; we adapt their task to tokens. Each sequence has 8 (symbol, label) pairs drawn from 32 symbols and 8 labels, with each symbol consistently paired with one label, followed by a query symbol from the context. With distinct symbols and a loss on the query label only, our models stayed at 0.325 accuracy for 20{,}000 steps, the level label counting reaches without induction. Longer training might escape this. We instead use 4 distinct symbols per sequence and a loss on the label of every repeated symbol; the no-induction ceiling is then 0.523, and induction forms in every run. The model is a 2-layer transformer (4 heads, $d_\text{model}{=}64$, MLP 256, 103k parameters) trained with AdamW (learning rate $10^{-3}$ throughout, batch 256), chosen by a $3{\times}3$ sweep on validation loss, as validation accuracy saturates.

\textbf{Collapse pipeline.} Generation 0 trains on 200{,}000 real sequences, generation $g$ on 200{,}000 with fresh real contexts and answers \emph{sampled} at temperature $T$ by generation $g{-}1$. The \emph{base} variant writes the query label; the \emph{extended} variant also writes a next symbol, whose true probability is $q(s){=}c/8$ for a symbol in $c$ of the 8 slots, and its label. Real contexts make the base target a deterministic function of an uncorrupted input, unlike \citet{shumailov2024collapse}, so one chain lets the model write whole sequences. We ran the base variant for 6 generations (25{,}000 steps) and extended chains for 8 (8{,}000 steps): $T{=}1$, $T{=}0.7$ and 10\% real data with five seeds each (paired: conditions share each seed's generation 0), plus 25{,}000 steps, generated contexts and accumulated data \citep{gerstgrasser2024inevitable}.

\textbf{Measures.} Every reported measure, circuit tests included, uses one set of 10{,}000 held-out real sequences; a separate validation set (5{,}120) only times the phase change. As $q$ is exact, so is the KL of the next-symbol distribution $p$, which splits in two:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{KL}(q\,\|\,p) = \underbrace{-\log(1-p_\text{out})}_{\text{leakage}} + \underbrace{\mathrm{KL}(q\,\|\,\tilde p)}_{\text{misallocation}},
\label{eq:split}
\end{equation}
where $p_\text{out}$ is the mass outside the context and $\tilde p$ is $p$ renormalised over it. As a null model, we assume each generation learns its training distribution exactly; sampling at $T$ then gives $p_g \propto p_0^{(1/T)^g}$. We use \emph{resample ablation} \citep{conmy2023automated} and \emph{path patching} \citep{goldowskydill2023localizing}, which resamples a layer-0 head only where layer 1 reads queries, keys or values, and score every intervention on both the query label and the next-symbol distribution. In the spirit of \citet{chen2026mechanistic}, we retrain fresh models (3 seeds) on generation-7 data with or without its context-contradicting rows.

\section{Results}
\looseness=-1
\textbf{No collapse in the base variant.} Every generation reaches query accuracy 1.000 on real data, and errors in the generated labels fall from $4.5{\times}10^{-5}$ to $5{\times}10^{-6}$: models copy the context instead of learning the sampled errors (supplementary Table~\ref{tab:train}).

\textbf{The extended variant degrades only in its next-symbol distribution.} Query and next-label accuracy on real data stay $\geq$0.997 in every generation of every chain, yet the next-symbol KL grows from 0.09 to 0.52 nats by generation 7 (medians of five seeds; range 0.49--0.56; supplementary Table~\ref{tab:collapse}). At $T{=}1$ leakage adds about generation 0's own error each generation (Fig.~\ref{fig:main}a), and misallocation among symbols in the context makes up 66--71\% of the KL (Fig.~\ref{fig:main}b). Training each generation 3$\times$ longer halves leakage (0.074 vs.\ 0.161 nats) but \emph{increases} misallocation (0.58 vs.\ 0.40 nats; one chain).

\textbf{Temperature decides which collapse occurs.} At $T{=}0.7$ leakage falls to 0.005--0.007 nats while misallocation reaches 2.5--2.8 nats in five seeds and single-slot symbols nearly vanish (0.017--0.027; true 0.125). Through generation 4 every seed stays within $-$0.05 to $+$0.18 nats of the null model, so sampling accounts for most of this narrowing. By generation 7 all lie 2.1--2.5 nats \emph{below} it (supplementary Table~\ref{tab:excess}). Keeping 10\% real data halves the KL (0.29--0.32) and caps misallocation near 0.17--0.21 while leakage keeps rising; accumulating data keeps misallocation at its generation-0 level and leakage at 0.059 nats (supplementary Table~\ref{tab:revision}).

\textbf{Filtering removes the expected failure only.} Context-contradicting rows (14.5\% of generation-7 data) are the only source of out-of-context targets, so deleting them restores real-data leakage (0.016--0.018 vs.\ 0.018--0.023). Misallocation remains (0.42--0.47), because it arises in rows whose targets agree with the context (Fig.~\ref{fig:main}c).

\textbf{Copying survives generated contexts.} A model that writes whole sequences still pairs $\geq$99.5\% of repeated symbols with their label (real query accuracy $\geq$0.9997), while the symbols it chooses for its contexts drift: context-symbol KL 0.18$\to$1.17 nats, and 8.3\% of queries leave the context.

\textbf{The query circuit survives.} In all 150 models, resampling layer 1 drops query accuracy to near chance (0.13--0.14; chance 0.125) and layer 0 to 0.20--0.44. Where one layer-0 head matters (128 models), its path into layer 1's \emph{keys} carries a median 99\% of its effect but the direct edge only 8\% (Fig.~\ref{fig:main}d): unlike \citet{singh2024needs}'s attention-only models, ours relay K-composition through the layer-0 MLP.

\textbf{Next-symbol prediction does not use the induction pathway.} Scored on the next-symbol distribution (144 models; supplementary Table~\ref{tab:choice}), patching the previous-token head into layer 1 changes the KL by at most 0.07 nats with real contexts (0.19 with generated ones), and the induction head matters no more than the other layer-1 heads, which serve as controls. Yet resampling layer 1 or layer 0 raises misallocation from 0.18 to 1.3--1.5 nats: the prediction depends on the same layers, but not through K-composition. At $T{=}0.7$, layer-1 attention stays near-uniform over the slots while the output sharpens.

\textbf{The next-symbol target makes induction form sooner.} Without the next-symbol target the phase change (validation accuracy $\geq$0.9) comes at 6{,}250--7{,}750 instead of 750--1{,}750 steps; collapsed data does not delay it.

\section{Discussion}
\looseness=-1
With real contexts the query label is a deterministic function of the input, so a model that learns induction ignores errors in its sampled training labels. This follows largely from the design, but also holds when the model writes its own contexts. The next-symbol distribution is different: matching $c/8$ requires counting how often each symbol occurs rather than copying a label, a computation the induction pathway does not perform. Our interventions identify a pathway that next-symbol prediction does not use, but not the full computation behind it. In our experiments, a model can pass the ICL accuracy tests, and the circuit tests built on them, while its next-symbol distribution degrades.

Eq.~\ref{eq:split} separates two failures. Leakage, probability on symbols outside the context, behaves like approximation error: longer training shrinks it and a consistency filter removes it. Misallocation, error among symbols in the context, arises in rows whose targets agree with the context and grows with longer training. This is consistent with each model fitting noise in its finite sample, the statistical error of \citet{shumailov2024collapse}; in our chains only real data limits it. At $T{=}0.7$ the shift from leakage to misallocation comes mostly from sampling through generation 4, as biased sampling trades diversity for quality \citep{alemohammad2024mad}, and per-sample verification \citep{feng2025beyond} addresses only leakage.

\textbf{Limitations.} Main conditions use five seeds, but the 25{,}000-step, generated-context and accumulated-data chains use one each. Chains are short (8 generations), generation is confounded with seed within a chain, the deletion test covers one chain, and our task adapts Singh et al.'s rather than reproducing it. The results concern a 2-layer, 103k-parameter model on a synthetic task, not induction circuits in large language models.
